I am a PhD researcher in the Department of Design, Manufacturing and Engineering Management at the University of Strathclyde. My research focuses on collaborative robotics for process-engineering and laboratory environments, with a central question: when does a robot become worth adopting in a real workflow, not just technically capable in isolation?

That question sits between robotics systems engineering and human-centred design. I work with evidence from human-robot collaboration, industrial task analysis, safety-aware autonomy, and human factors to define benchmarks that reflect real deployment constraints: material state, traceability, operator judgement, constrained access, uncertainty, and recovery from failure.

Before starting my PhD, I completed a First-Class MEng in Mechanical Engineering at Cardiff University, including an international exchange at KAIST in South Korea. At KAIST I studied robotics, reinforcement learning, control, and AI for physical systems. At Cardiff I focused on control, simulation, sustainable energy systems, and mechatronics, including a wearable navigation-aid project that combined RGB-D perception, visual SLAM, artificial potential field planning, haptic feedback, and user testing.

I also completed an industrial placement at Bosch Thermotechnology in the Quality department, where I worked across test-rig control, sensor integration, lifetime testing, and continuous improvement. That experience shaped how I think about engineering research: a system is only valuable if it survives the constraints of real users, real workflows, and real organisations.

---

## Current Focus

My current work is building an adoption-aware research frame for collaborative robotics in process engineering. Rather than asking only whether a robot can complete a manipulation task, I examine the task, human role, safety condition, workflow context, and evidence needed to justify adoption.

Near-term work includes a structured literature review, CPI-facing benchmark development, and experimental planning for task families such as preparative handling and constrained manipulation. Alongside this, I maintain a research-engine workflow that links papers, evidence cards, decisions, claims, KPIs, and benchmark requirements in a version-controlled knowledge base.
